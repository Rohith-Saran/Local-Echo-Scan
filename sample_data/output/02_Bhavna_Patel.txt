Aim: To verify Ohm's law by establishing the relationship between the potential difference across a given metallic conductor and the electric current flowing through it, and to determine the unknown resistance of the resistor by plotting a V-I graph.

Apparatus Required: A DC regulated power supply (0-15V), a digital DC voltmeter (0-20V), a digital DC ammeter (0-500mA), an unknown wire-wound resistor, a variable rheostat (0-100 ohms), a one-way plug key, and insulated connecting copper wires.

Procedure: Clean the ends of the connecting wires using sandpaper and assemble the circuit as per the circuit schematic. Connect the voltmeter in parallel across the resistor specimen and the ammeter in series with the battery and rheostat. Close the circuit key and adjust the rheostat slider to record initial values of voltage V and current I. Increase the potential systematically in regular increments, taking simultaneous readings on both meters at each step. Plot a graph with potential difference on the y-axis and current on the x-axis to calculate the slope representing resistance.

Observation and Tabulation: The readings obtained from the circuit were recorded as follows: At applied voltage 2.0 V, current was 0.040 A. At voltage 4.0 V, current was 0.081 A. At voltage 6.0 V, current was 0.120 A. At voltage 8.0 V, current was 0.159 A. At voltage 10.0 V, current was 0.201 A. The average ratio of V/I yields an empirical resistance value of approximately 49.8 ohms.

Calculations: From the linear regression slope of the V-I plot: Slope = Delta V / Delta I = (10.0 - 2.0) / (0.201 - 0.040) = 8.0 / 0.161 = 49.69 ohms. The percentage deviation from the nominal 50-ohm resistor is approximately 0.62%.

Conclusion: The experiment successfully verified Ohm's Law. The V-I characteristic curve was a straight line passing through the origin, confirming that the current flowing through the resistor is directly proportional to the applied voltage. The experimental resistance was found to be 49.7 ohms.
